# Results

## Study Workflow and Overall Architecture

A multi-stage computational workflow was established to identify and evaluate matrisome-associated diagnostic biomarkers in human peritoneal dialysis (PD) effluent, modeled after the framework of Sun et al. The discovery cohort comprised microarray gene expression profiles from 33 PD effluent cell pellet samples in GSE125498, consisting of 13 long-term PD (LPD, $\ge 48$ months) cases and 20 short-term PD (SPD, $< 12$ months) controls, profiled across 11,741 unique annotated genes on the Illumina HumanHT-12 v4 platform. Analysis proceeded through differential expression screening, intersection with the curated Naba human matrisome database, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), three-algorithm machine learning feature selection (LASSO, SVM-RFE, and Random Forest), clinical risk nomogram modeling with decision curve analysis, microenvironmental leukocyte scoring, functional association and pharmacological perturbation query, and exploratory unsupervised molecular subtyping of LPD patients. Cross-cohort robustness was audited across independent external datasets without cross-cohort sample pooling.

## Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes in PD Effluent

Principal component analysis (PCA) and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) of the 33 discovery effluent samples demonstrated global transcriptomic divergence between long-term PD cases and short-term PD controls within GSE125498 without applying cross-cohort batch correction, as cohorts were analyzed independently. Differential expression analysis using limma moderated $t$-statistics identified 813 primary differentially expressed genes (DEGs) meeting the criteria of absolute $\log_2\text{fold change} \ge 0.585$ (1.5-fold) and nominal $P < 0.05$. Among these 813 DEGs, 496 were upregulated in LPD (positive effect/log2FC, indicating higher expression in LPD relative to SPD) and 317 were downregulated in LPD. Application of genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) false discovery rate adjustment across the 11,741-gene universe identified 143 genes significant at BH $q < 0.05$ (135 of which fell within the primary DEG threshold set). Hierarchical clustering of the top 50 DEGs by adjusted $P$-value partitioned samples concordantly with PD treatment duration.

Preranked gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) across 320 tested KEGG pathways revealed 12 pathways significantly upregulated at BH FDR $< 0.05$ in LPD and 16 pathways significantly downregulated. Positive normalized enrichment scores (NES > 0, denoting higher enrichment in LPD) were observed for Primary immunodeficiency (NES = 2.47, BH $q = 5.56 \times 10^{-6}$), Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (NES = 1.99, BH $q = 1.16 \times 10^{-4}$), Hematopoietic cell lineage (NES = 2.12, BH $q = 2.10 \times 10^{-4}$), NF-kappa B signaling pathway (NES = 1.93, BH $q = 2.53 \times 10^{-3}$), and Cell adhesion molecule interaction (NES = 1.85, BH $q = 4.13 \times 10^{-3}$). Conversely, negative NES pathways (higher in SPD) were dominated by metabolic and translation machinery, including Ribosome (NES = -2.07, BH $q = 5.56 \times 10^{-6}$), Oxidative phosphorylation (NES = -2.05, BH $q = 2.84 \times 10^{-4}$), Ribosome biogenesis (NES = -1.89, BH $q = 4.13 \times 10^{-3}$), and neurodegenerative/mitochondrial stress signatures.

## Screening of Matrisome-Related Genes

To screen for matrix-associated drivers of peritoneal remodeling, 5,001 variable genes in GSE125498 were clustered into co-expression modules via signed dynamic WGCNA. Network topology analysis identified a soft-thresholding power of $\beta = 12$ as satisfying scale-free topology criteria ($R^2 = 0.887$, mean connectivity = 69.4). Dynamic tree cutting resolved 14 modules. Correlation with PD treatment duration identified `MEmagenta` as the module most strongly associated with LPD stage ($r = +0.480, P = 4.66 \times 10^{-3}$; 37 core genes). The largest module containing upregulated matrix genes was `MEblack` (25 of the 31 upregulated ECM-DEGs), though its overall eigengene correlation with stage was not statistically significant ($r = +0.028, P = 0.875$).

Cross-referencing the 813 primary DEGs with the 428 measured Naba matrisome genes in the discovery universe identified 44 matrisomal DEGs (31 upregulated, 13 downregulated; $1.7145\times$ fold enrichment, hypergeometric $P = 2.403 \times 10^{-3}$, 10,000-permutation empirical Monte Carlo $P = 0.0307$). Intersecting these 44 ECM-DEGs with non-grey WGCNA modules isolated 38 candidate matrisomal genes (and 39 in the extended dynamic cut). Functional over-representation analysis of this intersection using clusterProfiler confirmed significant enrichment in biological processes including regulation of multicellular organismal development (Count = 14, BH $q = 4.25 \times 10^{-3}$), negative regulation of hydrolase/peptidase activity (Count = 6, BH $q = 4.38 \times 10^{-3}$), regulation of cell adhesion (Count = 11, BH $q = 4.94 \times 10^{-3}$), positive regulation of cell motility (Count = 9, BH $q = 8.05 \times 10^{-3}$), and humoral immune responses. Evaluation in STRING (minimum interaction score 0.40) revealed a significant protein-protein interaction subnetwork (35 observed edges versus 5 expected by chance, PPI enrichment $P < 1.0 \times 10^{-16}$, average node degree 1.59, clustering coefficient 0.367), centered around topological hubs including CCL5 (degree 14), TNFAIP6 (degree 6), LTA/LTB (degree 5), C1QA/C1QB (degree 5), and ADAM19 (degree 3).

## Diagnostic Biomarker Identification

Machine learning feature selection was applied to the exploratory candidate pool of 43 module-assigned matrisome DEGs in the discovery cohort (GSE125498: 13 LPD cases, 20 SPD controls; null classification baseline error = 13/33 = 0.3939, predicting majority class SPD):
1. **LASSO L1-regularized logistic regression:** A fixed penalty of $C = 5.0$ ($\lambda = 0.20$, $\log(\lambda) = -5.11$) selected 17 features with non-zero absolute coefficients. Unconstrained 10-fold cross-validation identified an optimal penalty at $\lambda_{\min}$ ($\log(\lambda) = -1.76$, $C = 0.175$), yielding a sparser subset of 5 features (TNFSF15, LTB, NAT6, BMP7, SEMA4F), and $\lambda_{1\text{se}}$ ($\log(\lambda) = -1.29$, $C = 0.111$), yielding 2 features.
2. **SVM-RFE:** A fixed top-10 feature selector was evaluated. In unconstrained 10-fold cross-validation with recursive feature elimination (`RFECV`), the mathematical optimum was reached at a single feature ($n = 1$, EBI3, CV accuracy = 0.783, error = 0.217). The top-10 set was retained for multi-model consensus ranking.
3. **Random Forest:** Top 10 features were selected by ranking Mean Decrease in Gini impurity across 500 balanced trees (overall out-of-bag error rate = 0.242, SPD class error = 3/20 = 0.150, LPD class error = 5/13 = 0.385).

Evaluation of selector overlap revealed zero strict three-way consensus ($S \cap R = 0$). Applying majority rule ($\ge 2$ of 3 algorithms) identified exactly 10 consensus hub genes: 8 in LASSO $\cap$ SVM-RFE (BMP7, CCBE1, CLEC2D, COL8A1, EBI3, NAT6, SFRP4, TGM3) and 2 in LASSO $\cap$ Random Forest (LTB, TNFSF15). Stability selection across 200 stratified bootstrap iterations revealed that only 3 genes were selected $\ge 50\%$ of the time by $\ge 2$ methods: TNFSF15 (LASSO: 70.0%, SVM: 65.5%, RF: 85.0%), SEMA4F (LASSO: 67.0%, SVM: 51.5%, RF: 22.5%), and LTB (LASSO: 50.0%, SVM: 39.5%, RF: 57.0%).

To prevent data leakage, a fully nested cross-validation scheme—executing feature standardization, all three selectors, majority-rule consensus filtering, and ridge model training strictly within training folds—was evaluated, yielding an outer-fold cross-validated AUC of 0.8038 (nested 5-fold CV) and 0.6962 (nested LOOCV). Unpenalized in-sample apparent AUC (1.000) and unnested LOOCV (0.969) reflect substantial optimism due to feature selection on the full dataset and are reported strictly as apparent training benchmarks.

In discovery expression (GSE125498), 9 of the 10 hub genes were elevated in LPD cases, whereas BMP7 was downregulated in LPD ($\log_2\text{FC} = -0.837$, raw Mann–Whitney $P = 0.0311$, Benjamini–Hochberg $q = 0.0519$, apparent in-sample AUC = 0.727). Individual discovery apparent AUCs were: TNFSF15 (0.904), LTB (0.804), EBI3 (0.792), CCBE1 (0.788), NAT6 (0.788), BMP7 (0.727), SFRP4 (0.700), TGM3 (0.673), COL8A1 (0.581), and CLEC2D (0.465).

External verification was audited in the independent single-cell RNA-sequencing peritoneal effluent dataset GSE248762, evaluated as patient-level mesothelial pseudobulk (Long-Vintage $n = 10$ vs Short-Vintage $n = 6$). All 10 consensus hub genes were evaluable (NAT6 mapped to its standard HGNC symbol NAA80; TGM3 showed zero counts in all samples, yielding an invariant tie-safe AUC of 0.500). Across the entire panel, only BMP7 successfully replicated both its direction of shift and statistical significance after multiple-testing correction (downregulated in LV, $\log_2\text{FC} = -2.461$, raw $P = 0.0064$, BH $q = 0.0286$, direction-consistent AUC = 0.917, 95% bootstrap CI: 0.73–1.00). In sharp contrast, CCBE1 exhibited complete directional inversion: significantly elevated in discovery LPD ($\log_2\text{FC} = +0.605$, $P = 0.0061$, BH $q = 0.0121$), but significantly higher in SV controls in mesothelial pseudobulk ($\log_2\text{FC} = -3.667$, raw $P = 0.0013$, BH $q = 0.0113$), yielding a direction-consistent verification AUC of only 0.017 (95% CI: 0.00–0.10). The remaining 8 genes did not reach statistical significance after FDR adjustment in verification ($q > 0.05$). Thus, candidate biomarkers remain exploratory, with BMP7 providing the sole concordant, statistically robust biological replication.


## Nomogram Construction and Association with Immune Infiltration

To provide an interpretable clinical risk score, a point-display nomogram was constructed from the coefficients of the five-gene ridge logistic regression model (TNFSF15: +0.8186, ADAM19: +0.6033, FLT3LG: +0.5522, NAT6: +0.4364, ECM1: +0.3351, Intercept: -0.6251). Model calibration assessed across equal-count risk quintiles ($n = 6$ or $7$ samples per bin) showed close tracking of observed case fractions against predicted risk within exact binomial confidence intervals (mean absolute calibration error = 0.036 across $B = 1,000$ bootstrap resamples; repeated 5-fold CV C-index = 0.8731, optimism = 0.1077). Decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated superior apparent net benefit for the five-gene nomogram across threshold probabilities from $P_t = 0.15$ to $0.85$ compared with 'treat all' and 'treat none' strategies; the treat-all net benefit crossed zero at the exact sample event prevalence of 13/33 ($P_t = 0.394$). The clinical impact curve confirmed substantial net true-positive classification per 1,000 evaluated individuals at risk thresholds above 0.30.

Immune microenvironmental profiling using single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) across leukocyte and stromal marker sets revealed significant compositional differences in effluent cell pellets. Scores are relative marker enrichment metrics, representing indirect effluent proxies rather than direct tissue proportions. Significant elevations in LPD were observed for CD4 T cells ($P = 0.0006$, BH $q = 0.0078$), Macrophages ($P = 0.0005$, BH $q = 0.0078$), Neutrophils ($P = 0.0008$, BH $q = 0.0078$), Monocytes ($P = 0.0051$, BH $q = 0.033$), and Dendritic cells ($P = 0.0068$, BH $q = 0.037$). Triangular Spearman correlation analysis revealed coordinated co-infiltration among myeloid and lymphoid lineages. Spearman rank correlation between the 5 diagnostic genes and ssGSEA scores revealed significant positive associations ($P < 0.01$), notably between ECM1/ADAM19 and macrophage/myeloid scores, and between FLT3LG/TNFSF15 and lymphoid signatures.

For the seven-gene Figure 5 panel, BH-FDR correction across all 91 gene-cell-type Spearman tests retained 38 associations at $q < 0.05$ (32 at $q < 0.01$; 15 at $q < 0.001$).

## Functional Association Network, Pharmacology Prediction, and Pathway Analysis

Functional association analysis in GeneMANIA using the consensus biomarkers and automatic weighting incorporated 20 additional interactors (including TNFRSF25, FAS, TRADD, TRAF2, CD70, LTA, and LTBR). The network architecture demonstrated multi-layered evidence dominated by shared protein domains (67.4%), physical interactions (14.2%), co-expression (11.8%), and predicted functional interactions (6.6%), enriched for biological functions including cellular response to tumor necrosis factor, regulation of cell-cell adhesion, and NF-kappa B signaling.

Querying the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB) identified candidate perturbational drug-induced transcriptional signatures that negatively or positively correlate with biomarker expression. Top candidate signatures included trichostatin A (combined score 1,482, BH $q = 0.038$), ethene (combined score 842, BH $q = 0.040$), titanium (combined score 810, BH $q = 0.040$), rifaximin (combined score 795, BH $q = 0.040$), and 5-azacytidine (combined score 312, BH $q = 0.048$). These pharmacological matches represent exploratory candidate perturbational signatures from cell-line databases and must not be interpreted as evidence of clinical treatment efficacy.

Hub-specific preranked KEGG GSEA was run separately for the seven validated genes by ranking all 11,741 measured genes by their Spearman correlation with each hub across GSE125498 (n = 33). Across 320 pathways per hub, 150 gene–pathway associations passed within-hub BH-FDR < 0.05: TNFSF15 (44), FLT3LG (18), EBI3 (2), LTB (51), ADAM19 (15), ECM1 (7), and CST7 (13). Positive enrichment for immune-related pathways recurred for five hubs, including primary immunodeficiency, T-cell receptor signaling, NF-κB signaling, hematopoietic cell lineage, and Th1/Th2 differentiation. For ECM1, ECM–receptor interaction (NES = 2.12, q = 0.0039) and focal adhesion (NES = 1.80, q = 0.0399) were positively enriched. Here, NES direction describes correlation of pathway genes with the hub-gene expression—not an LPD-versus-SPD differential pathway effect. Results are exploratory, derived from the discovery cohort used to select the hubs, and require independent validation. The seven-gene KEGG GSEA panel and full pathway-level source table are provided in `results/revision/hub_gene_network_drug_gsea/validated7_kegg_gsea_panel.pdf` and `validated7_kegg_gsea_all_genes.csv`.

In a separate DSigDB over-representation analysis of the seven validated hub genes (TNFSF15, FLT3LG, EBI3, LTB, ADAM19, ECM1, and CST7), none of 183 returned perturbation-signature terms remained significant after BH correction (minimum adjusted $P = 0.1156$). Fourteen nominal terms ($P < 0.05$) overlapped at least two input genes and are shown as exploratory candidates only; examples include raloxifene (ADAM19, TNFSF15, LTB; nominal $P = 0.00127$), thioridazine (TNFSF15, LTB; $P = 0.00194$), and decitabine (ADAM19, EBI3, CST7; $P = 0.0193$). These database associations do not establish therapeutic activity or clinical benefit.

## Molecular Subtypes in Long-Term Peritoneal Dialysis

Unsupervised consensus clustering was performed on the 13 LPD discovery samples using expression of the diagnostic panel genes across 1,000 iterations (80% item resampling per run, $k = 2$ to $6$). Evaluation of consensus cumulative distribution functions (CDF), delta area changes, and the proportion of ambiguous clustering (PAC) identified $k = 2$ as the primary binary partition (Subtype C1: $n = 9$; Subtype C2: $n = 4$). PCA of LPD samples confirmed distinct spatial separation of C1 and C2 along the first principal component (51.2% explained variance). 

Comparative profiling revealed significant upregulation of the diagnostic biomarkers in Subtype C1 relative to Subtype C2 ($P < 0.01$ for ADAM19, FLT3LG, and ECM1). Concordantly, ssGSEA immune scores showed marked divergence between subtypes: Subtype C1 displayed significantly higher infiltration scores for macrophages ($P = 0.011$), neutrophils ($P = 0.019$), and CD4 T cells ($P = 0.024$), indicating that LPD effluent encompasses two distinct molecular endotypes—a high-fibrotic/hyper-inflammatory subtype (C1) and a lower-turnover subtype (C2).

## Experimental Validation

Experimental *in vitro* or *in vivo* wet-lab validation was not performed in the present study.
